## Reputation

Reputation runs from 0 to 100 and starts at 30. It goes up with renewals, delivered tenders, good **reviews** and awards, and down when customers leave or deadlines are missed. A higher reputation brings more and bigger requests and better odds in tenders. Customers write reviews about power, network, support and security; the stars follow the quality you deliver.

Managers sometimes send a **proposal**: better equipment from a fictional brand (router, DDoS protection, CRAH fans, UPS firmware, security tech, CRM). Approving it gives a lasting bonus; a proposal expires after 20 days.

## Security

The **security level** (0 to 100) is built from seven parts. Banks, municipalities and the government ask for a minimum.

| Part | What counts |
|---|---|
| Fence | A fence all round the site; electric after research |
| Gate | Guard booth, barrier, intercom, bollards; ANPR after research |
| Cameras | Camera poles outside, domes in halls, MMR and corridors |
| Access | Badge doors on all halls and the MMR; biometrics and mantrap after research |
| Security room | Security video wall and glass lodge |
| People | Guards (hired by the security manager) |
| Policies | ID check at the entrance, escort for visitors, visits only during office hours |

## Certificates

ISO 27001, Tier III, PCI DSS, the government seal and a green label. Each has requirements (security level, redundancy, policies, green power, PUE), an audit of 20 to 45 days and a price, and lasts three years with a yearly check. A compliance officer handles them. Government asks for the government seal plus ISO, banks for PCI, cloud, hyperscalers and AI for Tier III.

## Research

An **R&D lab** with lab benches (one researcher per bench) works through the research tree. A project costs money when you queue it and points to finish; set a daily **R&D budget** to go faster. Six branches:

| Branch | Examples |
|---|---|
| Power | Lithium batteries, eco UPS, heavy busbar, peak shaving |
| Cooling | Cold corridor, EC fans, free cooling, selling waste heat, liquid cooling (opens AI customers) |
| Network | Coherent optics, SDN, DDoS protection |
| Construction | Prefab, longer rows, logistics |
| Security | Biometrics, ANPR, mantrap, electric fence, AI video |
| Business | CRM, SLA packages, ESG, government market, office rental |

## Growth

Everything about growing lives under **Growth** (<kbd>F7</kbd>):

- **Bigger campus**: buy land up to 96, 128, 192 or 256 metres square. The plot grows to the north and west; what you built stays where it is.
- **More sites and countries**: with an international director you open countries (Belgium, Germany, France, Ireland, Denmark, Sweden, Spain), each with its own energy price, build cost, demand and grid wait. A site is built from a template (edge, medium, large, hyperscale) and runs itself with a local team. Link sites with your own fiber.
- **Office rental**: the office manager rents empty offices, first to customers already in your datacenter.
- **Your own hosting company**: VPS, dedicated servers, GPU, storage and backup on racks in "own cloud" halls. Servers are ordered, prepared at the warehouse workbenches and installed by hosting engineers.
- **Market**: rivals grow and bid on tenders, sometimes a datacenter is for sale, and with enough revenue you can **go public**. A news ticker at the bottom shows what happens.
- **Energy and ESG**: hourly energy prices, solar, wind, battery containers, dry coolers that save water, and an ESG score.
- **Risks**: cyber attacks (stopped by a SOC), disasters (storm, lightning, flood, substation failure, fire) with **insurance**, and supply delays.
- **Key people**: a headhunter finds well-known names with a lasting bonus. Rivals will try to poach them.
